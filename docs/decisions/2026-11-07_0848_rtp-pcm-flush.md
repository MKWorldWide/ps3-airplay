## Architecture / logic changes

On successful RTSP FLUSH, the receiver clears queued RTP frames, drains pending audio datagrams up to a fixed 256-packet cap, resets sequence and retransmission epoch fields, and increments `flush_generation`. The poll loop avoids consuming an audio readiness event captured before the flush. The standalone app sees the generation change and clears PCM and interpolation state without resetting cumulative counters. The RTP and PCM clear functions preserve lifetime metrics.

## Runtime behavior changes

After a successful FLUSH, stale queued RTP and PCM are discarded before subsequent audio rendering. The receiver logs RTP queue/socket discards, a drain-cap event when applicable, and PCM frame discards. Empty UDP datagrams are consumed as packets rather than ending the bounded drain early.

## Known limitations / follow-ups

- The datagram drain is intentionally capped; `RTP_FLUSH_DRAIN_LIMIT` reports remaining queued data. Add cap-exhaustion coverage and confirm sender behavior under overload.
- This change does not implement sender-clock synchronization, volume, stable network routing, or complete ALAC interoperability.
- v0.1.15 package generation and RPCS3 route repair remain future steps.
- No iPhone session, streamed RTP, decoded stream audio, or Spotify playback has been demonstrated.

## Risks introduced

The FLUSH path discards buffered data by design. A hostile or continuously sending peer could refill the socket; per-invocation work remains bounded to 256 datagrams. A queue exceeding the cap is logged and could require further draining.

## Decision record

### Decision 1: Coordinate FLUSH across all audio buffering layers

- **Context:** Session acknowledgment alone left RTP queue, kernel socket, PCM ring, and interpolation history untouched.
- **Options considered:** Clear only the session retransmission range; clear the RTP queue; clear every layer and expose a generation to the app loop.
- **Why chosen:** All layers can retain pre-seek audio. A generation counter gives the app a simple, deterministic synchronization point without moving audio ownership into the receiver thread.
- **Tradeoffs accepted:** The receiver maintains a small generation field; buffered samples are intentionally dropped, and socket draining is bounded.

### Decision 2: Keep the datagram drain bounded

- **Context:** Draining until empty without a cap could monopolize the receiver if packets continue arriving.
- **Options considered:** Unbounded drain; one capped drain with diagnostics; persistent multi-poll drain state.
- **Why chosen:** One fixed 256-packet cap keeps each RTSP handling step finite and preserves diagnostics for the exceptional case.
- **Tradeoffs accepted:** If the cap is exhausted, remaining stale datagrams may require a later refinement; the event is logged and this limitation is documented.

### Decision 3: Interpret the emulator run at its earliest failed stage

- **Context:** RPCS3 briefly displayed the app, then returned to its list.
- **Options considered:** Treat screen initialization as success; blame mDNS sharing; follow the fresh log's readiness order.
- **Why chosen:** The capped log ends at the bounded IP lookup timeout, before any receiver listener or mDNS setup.
- **Tradeoffs accepted:** The SO_REUSEPORT behavior remains unknown until an environment with usable route and multicast support is tested.
